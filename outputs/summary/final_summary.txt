1. RESEARCH QUESTIONS & CORE OBJECTIVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project explores the behavioral patterns and demographic factors associated with 
current alcohol use (within the past 30 days) among US high school students using the 
CDC's YRBS 2007 dataset. The analysis investigates two primary dimensions:
  - Sex Dynamics: Is there a statistically significant association between biological 
    sex and alcohol consumption?
  - Age Development: Does alcohol consumption follow a significant chronological or 
    developmental trajectory across adolescence (ages 12 to 18)?


2. ANALYTICAL VARIABLE DEFINITIONS (DATA DICTIONARY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To ensure full data integrity and reproducibility, raw categorical response codes 
were rigorously parsed (handling missing values via .dropna() and wiping invisible 
BOM characters using 'utf-8-sig' encoding) into the following structured framework:

  - Sex_Binary (Independent Variable): 
    * Type: Binary Categorical (0/1)
    * Mapping: 0 = Male (n = 6,213), 1 = Female (n = 6,402)
  
  - Age_Numeric (Independent Variable):
    * Type: Discrete Numeric Integer (Focused on the core cohort of ages 12 to 18)
    * Mapping: Raw codes 1-7 mapped sequentially to chronological ages 12-18.

  - Alcohol_Binary (Dependent Response Variable):
    * Type: Binary Categorical (0/1)
    * Mapping: 1 = Current Alcohol Use (Active user in past 30 days; raw codes 2-7)
               0 = No Current Use (Abstinent/0 days in past 30 days; raw code 1)


3. INFERENTIAL STATISTICAL METHODOLOGY & ASSUMPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A dual Chi-Square (χ²) Test of Independence framework was chosen over standard 
two-proportion z-tests to elegantly handle the multi-level nature of age data and 
maintain methodological unity across sex-based comparisons.

All statistical assumptions were fully verified:
  - Independence: Multi-stage cluster sampling design ensures distinct individual samples.
  - 10% Condition: Analytical sample (12,615) is <10% of the US high school population.
  - Large Sample Expectation: Due to the robust sample size, all expected frequencies 
    in the cross-tabulation cells vastly exceed the standard rule-of-thumb threshold (E_ij >= 5).


4. KEY STATISTICAL FINDINGS & VISUAL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Test 1: Sex vs. Alcohol Consumption (Overall Sample)
  * Findings: Overall Male Alcohol Use Rate = 45.74% | Female Alcohol Use Rate = 44.53%
  * Statistics: Chi-Square Statistic = 1.8152, df = 1, p-value = 0.1779
  * Conclusion: Since p > 0.05, we fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is no 
    statistically significant association between biological sex and current alcohol use 
    overall. The observed 1.21% raw variance is strictly attributed to random sampling error.
  
  * VISUAL BACKING (stacked_alcohol_gender.png):
    The 100% stacked bar chart visually displays the behavior composition between 
    genders. The vertical proportion of active alcohol users (45.74% for Males vs. 
    44.53% for Females) and non-users shows a nearly identical structural breakdown, 
    graphically illustrating why the raw variance is practically negligible.
    
  * MATHEMATICAL PROOF (confidence_interval_diff.png):
    This sensitivity analysis robustly validates the "Fail to Reject H0" decision. 
    It compares our sample's empirical Chi-Square value (dashed line at 1.8152) against 
    the critical threshold bars at multiple confidence levels (80%, 90%, 95%, 99%). 
    The empirical line is far to the left, failing to clear even the lowest barrier 
    (80% CI critical threshold). This provides bulletproof visual evidence that the 
    1.21% gap is entirely due to background sampling fluctuation and holds no statistical weight.

- Test 2: Age vs. Alcohol Consumption (Developmental Stratification)
  * Findings: Alcohol consumption rates demonstrate an explosive, dramatic surge as 
    adolescents age (e.g., hovering around ~33% at age 14, but skyrocketing to 53.17% by age 18).
  * Statistics: Chi-Square Statistic = 202.4067, df = 6, p-value < 0.0001
  * Conclusion: With p < 0.0001, we strongly reject the null hypothesis. There is an 
    exceptionally significant statistical association between chronological age and alcohol use.

- Crucial Interaction Insight (The Gender-Age Cross-over Effect):
  While the macro-level sex test yielded non-significant results, the fine-grained multi-variable 
  breakdown uncovers a hidden psychological transition phase across developmental stages:
  * Early Adolescence (Ages 14-15): Female students exhibit a higher propensity for 
    alcohol use than males (e.g., Age 14 Female: 38.15% vs. Male: 27.23%).
  * Late Adolescence (Ages 17-18): Male students experience a sharper acceleration and 
    ultimately overtake females as they reach adulthood (e.g., Age 18 Male: 55.53% vs. Female: 50.54%).
